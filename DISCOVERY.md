# Discovery call checklist — Petlong portfolio site

Goal of the call: leave with enough raw material to build a 3-page site without needing a second round of questions.

Rule of thumb: I can write copy, choose type, and design layout. She is the only source for facts, names, dates, numbers, and photos.

---

## 1. Identity — the 5 things every visitor needs in 5 seconds

- [ ] **Exact display name.** Is "Petlong" a first name, a nickname, or the brand name? What goes in the header, what goes in the `<title>` tag, and is there a surname to include?
- [ ] **One-line role.** This is the single most important sentence on the site. Options she might pick from: sustainability consultant / strategist / analyst / researcher / founder / programme manager / student. Get her words, not mine.
- [ ] **Voice.** First person ("I work on…") or third person ("Petlong is driven by…")? The LinkedIn text is third person, which usually means someone else wrote it. Ask which she wants. First person is warmer and shorter; third person suits a consultancy brand.
- [ ] **What the site is for.** Job hunting, freelance clients, or personal brand/thought leadership? This decides everything: "hire me" vs "work with me" vs "here's my thinking."
- [ ] **Location, languages, availability.** City, working languages, and whether she wants to advertise that she's open to work.

## 2. The work — this is 80% of the site

Ask for **3 to 6 projects.** Fewer, described well, beats a long list. For each one get:

- [ ] Title / short name for the project
- [ ] Who it was for (organisation, client, or "coursework" if academic). Real names only if she's allowed to use them.
- [ ] **Her role**, precisely. "Led the stakeholder mapping" is useful. "Was involved in" is not.
- [ ] The problem in one or two sentences
- [ ] What she actually did and what she produced (a report? a framework? a pilot? a policy?)
- [ ] **The outcome, with a number if one exists** — tonnes diverted, hectares, budget, headcount reached, % reduction, adoption rate
- [ ] Dates / duration
- [ ] Why she's proud of it, in her own words — this is often the best copy on the page
- [ ] **Confidentiality check.** Any NDA, employer policy, or client restriction? Ask this explicitly, per project. It's the most common thing that stalls a portfolio build.

**Numbers need sources.** In sustainability, an unsourced impact claim is a liability and a sharp visitor will notice. For every figure, ask: where did it come from, and who published it first?

## 3. The story — one real paragraph

- [ ] **Why sustainability.** Not the LinkedIn line — that one is generic and could belong to anyone. Ask for the origin: a place, a course, a job, a problem she couldn't let go of. One honest paragraph about a specific thing beats three about "responsibility and opportunity."
- [ ] **What she wants to be known for.** If a reader remembers one idea from the site, what is it?

## 4. Proof and credentials

- [ ] CV / résumé file (PDF)
- [ ] LinkedIn URL, and any other profiles worth linking (Google Scholar, ORCID, GitHub, ResearchGate)
- [ ] Degrees, institutions, years. Exact spelling of the institution and the degree title.
- [ ] Certifications and frameworks she's trained in — GRI, SASB, ISSB, CSRD, GHG Protocol, ISO 14001, LEED, B Corp, etc.
- [ ] Publications, reports, talks, panels, podcasts, press mentions. Anything with a URL or a PDF.
- [ ] Awards, grants, fellowships
- [ ] Volunteer or community work she wants shown (or deliberately left off)
- [ ] **2–3 testimonials.** Names, job titles, and a written quote or permission to contact them. Real quotes only — a made-up quote is the fastest way to lose trust.

## 5. Visual assets

- [ ] **Photos of her.** At least one clean headshot, plus any candid/working shots. Higher resolution than LinkedIn, and she must own or have permission to publish them. Ask if she's comfortable being on the site at all, or wants a type-only site.
- [ ] Project images: photos, diagrams, charts, screenshots. Confirm she owns them.
- [ ] Logo or wordmark, if one exists. If not, a wordmark set in a typeface is the minimalist answer, and I'll handle it.
- [ ] Any existing brand colours (e.g. from a LinkedIn banner) or typeface she likes. Also: any employer brand she must not clash with.
- [ ] Style she likes and, more usefully, style she **dislikes**. Send her two or three example sites to react to.

## 6. Technical and practical

- [ ] **Domain.** Does she own one? If not, we pick on the call — check availability of a few name variants. Decide who pays for it and whose account it's registered under (make it hers, not yours).
- [ ] **Contact email** to publish. Which address, and does she want it obfuscated or behind a form?
- [ ] **Social links.** Exact handles, and which ones she'd rather not show. Also check for any old account she's embarrassed by.
- [ ] **Contact form or plain email link?** Minimalist answer is a mailto link — no backend, nothing to break. Confirm she's fine with that.
- [ ] **Analytics: yes or no.** Worth noting that a reader who cares about sustainability might appreciate a no-tracking, no-cookie site. Ask her stance; it's a cheap brand win.
- [ ] **Does she want to update it herself?** A frozen site is much simpler and cheaper. A blog or news section means a CMS or a markdown workflow, and she needs to agree to actually write posts. Ask how often she'd realistically publish. If the answer is "rarely," skip it.
- [ ] Dark mode, accessibility needs, mobile-first assumptions — low priority but cheap to ask now.

## 7. Constraints

- [ ] **Deadline, and why it exists.** A job application closing date or a conference changes the scope drastically.
- [ ] Anything legally or contractually off-limits
- [ ] Who signs off on copy, and how many review rounds. Get a name.
- [ ] Budget, if any, for domain, hosting, and fonts. (Static hosting can be free; licensed fonts are the usual cost.)

## 8. Files to have her send before the call

Without these the call stalls:

1. CV (PDF)
2. Headshot and any other photos (original files, not screenshots)
3. Any project PDFs, decks, diagrams, or reports she's allowed to share
4. Certificate or credential names/PDFs
5. Testimonial text, or contact details for the people who'd give one
6. The full LinkedIn profile text, so we can see the whole thing, not just the tagline

## 9. Decisions I need on the call

- [ ] Site structure: single long page, or three pages (Home / Work / About)? I'll bring both layout options and mock them up.
- [ ] First vs third person
- [ ] Which 3–6 projects make the cut
- [ ] Domain name
- [ ] Whether there's a writing/blog section

## The main risk

The LinkedIn paragraph is written in generalities. It makes no falsifiable claim and cites nothing, so a visitor can't tell her apart from a thousand other sustainability graduates. A minimalist site amplifies whatever it shows, so if it shows only that paragraph, it amplifies nothing.

Priority order on the call: projects with outcomes → one real origin paragraph → credentials → photos. Everything else can be filled in later.
